# LinkedIn post ideas, week of 20 October 2026

Author: Faizan Akhtar, Junior AI Automation Consultant
Positioning: AI handles the 80 percent, humans do the important 20 percent
Goal: move past 620 followers and attract AI job offers in India

Style rules applied: no emojis, no dashes, no clichés, plain human tone.

## Idea 1. The job search agent that runs before I wake up

First line:
"I stopped opening job boards three weeks ago. A script does it at 7am and leaves me a shortlist."

What the post covers:
1. What the agent actually does each morning: reads a list of company career pages, pulls new roles, scores each one against my skills and location, and rewrites my CV for the ones worth applying to.
2. The part people get wrong: the scoring rules took longer to build than the scraping. I show the rough criteria I use and why a bad score is more useful than a good one.
3. What is still mine to do: the final read, the cover note, the decision to apply. That is the 20 percent, and the agent is not close to touching it.

Why it works: it is a live artifact, not an opinion, and recruiters reading it see the exact thing they would be hiring.

## Idea 2. What four years on Dubai construction sites taught me about automation

First line:
"Before I wrote a single line of automation, I spent four years chasing HVAC contractors across Dubai sites with a clipboard."

What the post covers:
1. Site coordination is process mapping with consequences. You learn where work actually stalls, who is waiting on whom, and which step nobody owns. That is the same skill automation needs.
2. The specific habit that carried over: before building anything, write out the manual process step by step and find the one step that repeats daily with no judgment in it. That step is the candidate.
3. Why people from operations backgrounds tend to build better tools than people who start from the tech. They already know the process is messier than the diagram.

Why it works: it makes a non technical background an asset rather than something to explain away, and it is a story only he can tell.

## Idea 3. Most people automate the wrong 80 percent

First line:
"Automating your client outreach does not save you time if the bottleneck was never the outreach."

What the post covers:
1. A short breakdown of the client acquisition system I am building: where leads come from, what gets researched automatically, what gets drafted, and the single point where I step in.
2. The test I run before automating anything: does this task repeat at least weekly, does it have a clear right answer, and would a wrong output cost me a relationship. If the last one is yes, a human keeps it.
3. Two things I tried to automate and pulled back, and what the failures taught me about where judgment actually sits in a sales process.

Why it works: it argues against the obvious take, which earns comments, and it shows restraint rather than hype.

## Suggested order and spacing

1. Monday: Idea 1. Concrete and specific, best opener for the week.
2. Wednesday: Idea 3. Opinion post, highest comment potential mid week.
3. Friday: Idea 2. Personal background story, reads well going into the weekend.
